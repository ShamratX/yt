# Step 2 Guide: How to Research a Topic and Verify Facts

> **Goal:** Build every video on **3 to 6 real, verifiable studies or historical sources** so viewers trust your channel—and never publish AI hallucinations.

---

## Why Research Is Your Unfair Advantage
In our competitor research, the highest-performing AI-assisted channels (`@Inkexplainer96`, `@Basically-Primitive`, `@HistoryInMinsYT`) **all cite real academic papers in their video descriptions**. Viewers comment positively on the accuracy, share the videos more often, and trust the channel enough to subscribe.

---

## The Simple 4-Step Fact-Check Process (30–45 Minutes)

### 1. Run the Topic Research Prompt
- Copy `03_Topic_Ideas/Prompt_01_Topic_Research.md` into Claude or ChatGPT (with web search enabled) and insert your `[VIDEO_TOPIC]`.
- Ask for: (a) the common myth, (b) the surprising evolutionary/historical truth, (c) 4–6 real named studies/scientists/years, and (d) any scientific debate or caveat.

### 2. Verify Every Single Source Yourself (CRITICAL)
AI models sometimes invent study titles, years, or statistics. Spend 15 minutes checking each source:
- Search the exact scientist name + year on **Google Scholar** (`scholar.google.com`), **PubMed** (`pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`), **Smithsonian Magazine**, **Nature**, or **Britannica**.
- Confirm the study actually exists and actually says what your notes claim.
- **If you cannot verify a statistic in 2 minutes, DELETE IT.** Never guess numbers.

### 3. Collect 1 "Wait, Really?" Fact + 1 Real Site/Fossil/Experiment
Every strong video needs concrete anchors, not vague statements:
- *Vague (Bad):* "Scientists proved ancient humans slept differently."
- *Concrete (Good):* "In 2015, UCLA anthropologist Jerome Siegel tracked sleep across three pre-industrial societies—the Hadza of Tanzania, the San of Namibia, and the Tsimané of Bolivia—using wristwatch sensors."

### 4. Save Your Verified Source List
- Save the verified bullet notes and clickable links into your episode folder: `01_script/research_notes.md`.
- Later, you will paste these exact links into your YouTube video description under `RESEARCH & SOURCES`.
