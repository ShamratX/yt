# Channel Art Style & Recurring Character Guide (`05_characters`)

> **Why This Folder Exists:** Competitor channels (`@Inkexplainer96`, `@Basically-Primitive`, `@Neon-Rush-3D`, `@AncestorFiless`) all use generic plain white-circle stickmen. Our channel wins visually by locking ONE elevated, memorable **editorial ink-and-watercolor illustration style** while ensuring **every individual scene is 100% original**.

---

## 1. Master Channel Art Style (Minimal, Clean & Uncluttered 2D)
- **Art Medium:** **Minimal, clean flat 2D editorial ink illustration** — crisp thin-to-medium charcoal outlines (`#1E2228`), smooth flat solid fills, generous negative/breathing space, and **zero visual clutter**.
- **Strict Color Discipline (NO Messy Multi-Color Chaos!):**
  - Use **maximum 2 to 3 colors per scene** (1 clean background + 1 charcoal ink line + 1 single accent color to guide the viewer's eye!).
  - **Day / Science / Modern Scenes:** Clean off-white cream background (`#F6F1E7`) + charcoal ink (`#1E2228`) + **ONE accent color** (either Terracotta `#C85A32` OR Muted Sage `#4E6E58`).
  - **Night / Cave / Prehistoric Scenes:** Clean muted midnight slate background (`#25344A`) + crisp light/charcoal linework + **ONE warm accent** (Soft Amber `#E59A3C`).
- **Strictly Forbidden:** Busy multi-color messy paintings, heavy watercolor splatters, crowded chaotic backgrounds, plain white-circle stick figures, glossy 3D plastic renders, photorealistic AI faces, neon gradients, or garbled AI text.

---

## 2. Story-Specific Characters vs. Optional Recurring Anchors
**Core Rule — Same Art Style, Different Characters Per Script:**
- **What stays the SAME:** Your channel's **editorial ink-and-watercolor art style**, charcoal line weight, and color palette.
- **What CHANGES in every script:** The **characters themselves**. Draw whatever diverse, story-specific people fit that episode's script (e.g., sleep scientists, doctors, tired office workers, teenagers, prehistoric hunters, tribal elders, medieval villagers, mothers, historical figures) — **never** force the exact same single character into every scene!
- The two optional archetypes below are ONLY for scenes where you want a generic everyday modern or ancient viewer stand-in:

### Character A — "The Modern Human" (Relatable Everyday Viewer Stand-In)
- **Visual Design:** Simple, stylized 2D editorial illustrated adult (NOT a stick figure — natural simplified proportions, expressive eyes/eyebrows, messy dark charcoal hair).
- **Signature Outfit:** Muted **terracotta/rust casual sweater** (`#C85A32`) or **sage-green hoodie** (`#4E6E58`) with dark trousers—instantly recognizable in every modern-mismatch scene.
- **Role:** Appears in modern everyday moments (waking up at 3 AM, staring at a glowing phone, sitting at an office desk, eating a donut, standing nervously on a stage).

### Character B — "The Ancestor" (Prehistoric Counterpart)
- **Visual Design:** Same crisp charcoal ink-and-watercolor facial style (creates instant visual "same human, different century" connection!).
- **Signature Outfit:** Earth-toned woven linen/leather tunic and muted fur wrap (sage/ochre/brown tones) with practical Stone Age tools (bone needle, obsidian spear, woven basket, glowing ember carrier).
- **Role:** Appears in prehistoric survival, campfire, ocean-crossing, and evolutionary scenes—showing our ancestors as **clever, observant, resourceful humans**, never silly caveman caricatures.

*(Tip: Once you generate 1 great reference image of Character A and 1 of Character B in Leonardo.ai, save them right here in `05_characters/` as `modern_human_ref.png` and `ancestor_ref.png`, and upload them as your Character/Style Reference for future scenes!)*

---

## 3. Mandatory Original-Visual Rules (Zero Repetitive AI Slop!)
Every single episode must follow these non-negotiable standards:
1. **Create original visuals specifically for each video** — never copy/paste old scenes from previous uploads.
2. **Avoid repetitive AI-generated templates** and generic stock-looking imagery.
3. **Give every scene a unique visual composition:** Rotate camera angles (wide landscape, over-the-shoulder, close-up detail, bird's-eye map, cross-section diagram, split-screen Modern vs. Ancient).
4. **Match visuals directly to the exact spoken narration** — never pad with unrelated filler backgrounds.
5. **Do not copy competitor videos, scripts, thumbnails, or artwork.**
6. **Stay historically & scientifically accurate** — no misleading visuals (e.g., no dinosaurs next to humans, no fake exaggerated anatomy).
7. **Keep a consistent channel art style** while ensuring every scene tells its own fresh visual story.
